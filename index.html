How the Chebyshev tutorial was made

An eight-page tutorial reconstructed from Dirac’s 19-minute Riemann Hypothesis explainer. The full YouTube transcript supplied the outline and examples. Four captured video frames and Rubinstein’s working guide supported the mathematical exposition.

Four steps from video to tutorial

  1. Read the full YouTube transcript

    YouTube’s Show transcript panel supplied the timestamped narration. I read the full sequence and used the seven chapter starts to organize the tutorial. I inspected the player at selected timestamps to check the on-screen math.

  2. Capture four frames

    I captured frames showing the explicit formula, the missing linear term in the ramp sum, the factorial’s rounding loss, and the lattice-index inequality. Each frame sits beside its corresponding derivation in the PDF.

  3. Expand the analytic arguments

    I worked out the residue that gives the exact baseline, wrote out Landau’s positivity argument, and used Rubinstein’s version 2 guide to prove the extension to windows. The guide takes its central arithmetic inequality from the Lean development.

  4. Recompute examples and check the PDF

    A prime sieve reproduced the video’s numerical example. I made two original plots, one of the normalized prime-sum error and one of the derivative kernels that localize the window argument. I compiled and rendered all eight PDF pages to check layout and legibility.

Reproducing the million-scale example

For each integer n, the sieve uses the weight Λ(n) = log p when n is a power of a prime p, and zero otherwise. I summed those weights against the ramp and subtracted the exact baseline.

G(x)= ∑n≤x Λ(n) (xn−1) −xlog⁡x +(1+γ)x

Here γ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. The calculation gave G(106) ≈ 11.0404, in agreement with the video’s example. The numerical agreement checks the implementation of the ramp and centering. The zero-free conclusion comes from the analytic argument and the arithmetic bound.
